Fake TDAC websites: how to spot one, and what to do if you paid

Checked 5 November 2026 by Darius Cheung. Independent guide, not a government website.

Short answer

The official TDAC is free and lives at tdac.immigration.go.th. Any other address, or any request for a card payment to submit the form, means you aren't on the official site. If you paid one, you may not have a valid card: file again on the official site, ask your bank about the charge, and watch out for messages using the details you gave them.

How to check in ten seconds

  • Read the address from the right. What matters is the part right before the first slash. It must end in immigration.go.th. A name such as tdac-thailand.example.com isn't official, even if the page shows a Thai crest.
  • Look for a price. The embassy says the card is free and warns against websites charging administrative fees.
  • Be wary of "urgent" or "fast approval". The card isn't an approval, and it can't be sped up by paying.
  • Don't arrive by ad. Type the address, or use a link from a Thai government or embassy site.

How common is this?

Thai immigration police warned about fake TDAC sites on the day the system launched, 1 May 2025, and said they look official, charge fees and collect sensitive data. In June 2025, immigration, the Tourist Police and embassies ran a campaign urging travellers to use only the official address, attributing part of the problem to scammers manipulating search results.

One account in The Thaiger describes a family of three who paid US$120 each for an "urgent service" and waited hours with no card. Note that article's content comes from a company selling related services, so treat it as one side's story.

If you paid or typed your details into a copy

  1. Assume you don't have a valid card. Go to the official site and file. It's free. If you have a card from the other site, compare it, but don't rely on it.
  2. Call your bank about the charge and ask what they can do. Have the date, amount and site address ready, and take screenshots if the page is still up.
  3. Treat your passport details as exposed. Expect convincing emails. Don't open links in messages about your "application", and don't give more details to anyone who contacts you.
  4. Check what's stored. If you created an account with a password you use elsewhere, change it there.
  5. Report it. Thailand's Tourist Police took part in the 2025 campaign, so they are a sensible place to report it.

Questions people ask

Is there a TDAC app I should use?

The embassy's instructions point to the website and its QR code. Be careful with apps from search results, and check who publishes them.

Is tdac.immigration.go.th safe?

That's the address the Thai embassy gives. As ever, check the spelling carefully, since copies often differ by a word.
